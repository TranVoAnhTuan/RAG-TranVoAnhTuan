## Chapter 1 -Compliance

## 1. Lender

Resimac employees are required to comply with all relevant laws and regulations including, but not limited to, the Corporations Law, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act (ASIC), the Trade Practices Legislation,  the  Privacy  Act  1988,  Anti-Money  Laundering  and  Counter-Terrorism  Financing  Act  2006 (AML/CTF) and the National Consumer Credit Protection Act 2009 (NCCP Act).

No Resimac employee is to use or disclose confidential information outside of the Resimac Group, about the Resimac Group or its customers, gained in the course of his or her employment unless compelled to do so by a Court of competent jurisdiction.

No Resimac employee is to act on any matter that specifically gives benefit or financial gain to themself, spouse or any other family members or any joint venture, partnership or corporation in which they have any interest or share. This includes being involved in any aspect of the origination, approval or ongoing management.

The Lending Procedures and Underwriting Guidelines Manual should also be read in conjunction with other Resimac Limited policy mandates dealing with the area of Risk and Compliance.

## 1.1 Accountability

Should a Resimac employee act outside their decision making authority, act outside or ignore policy, ignore approval terms and conditions, falsify entries or records or fail to disclose any relevant information relating to an applicant, it will adversely affect their employment with Resimac.

Each indiscretion will be reviewed on a case by case basis with the consequence being dependent on the level of seriousness.

## 1.2 Anti-Discrimination &amp; Code of Conduct (Trade Practices Act)

Resimac is committed to ensuring that the lending policies and underwriting guidelines do not discriminate against applicant(s), Mortgage Originators (Mortgage Managers), Aggregators and Direct Introducers, or any other associated party.

Resimac employees should  refer  to  Resimac  policy  mandates  for  situations  leading  to  discrimination  and expected code of conduct, which extends to lending activities.

In summary, employees must not:

- Engage in any conduct, or make any representation that is false, misleading, unfair, unreasonable, or prone to misinterpretation.
- Enter into any transaction where the parties involved do not or cannot understand the consequences.
- Exert undue influence, or employ unfair tactics, in all dealings with applicant(s) / Mortgage Originators (Mortgage Managers)/ Aggregators / Direct Introducers.

## 1.3 Uniform Consumer Credit Code 1996 (UCCC)

The National Consumer Credit Protection Act will supersede the UCCC for all loan contracts entered into from July 1, 2010.

This is a summary of the key aspects of the UCCC in Australia and policies that employees involved in the credit analysis process within Resimac are required to comply with (where applicable):

- Loans for either regulated or unregulated purposes as defined by the UCCC are permitted. The type of security taken is irrelevant.
- The  UCCC  regulates  lending  to  individuals  where  the  money  is  provided  wholly  or  predominantly  for personal, domestic or household purposes. Conversely, if the majority (i.e. more than 50%) of the funds are used for business purposes, the loan is not regulated.
- A Declaration as to Purpose of Credit is to be signed by the applicant(s) if the predominant purpose of funds is for business and / or investment.

All loans are to be treated as if they are regulated in terms of due diligence and care. The fact that a loan may be unregulated, should not impact the standard of credit analysis applied, legal documentation and settlement of loans respectively. It is a requirement under the UCCC that an applicant must receive a beneficial interest from the proceeds of the loan. If a party to the loan is not receiving a benefit from the loan they cannot become a borrower and the matter is to be referred to Risk Management for further consideration.

## 1.4 The National Consumer Credit Protection Act 2009 (NCCP Act)

The NCCP Act replaces the UCCC with the new National Credit Code (NCC) from July 1, 2010. The NCC is a schedule to the NCCP Act.

The NCCP Act has been introduced to provide a national set of rules and regulation for the consumer credit industry. The NCCP Act requires that unsuitable credit is not arranged for consumers and that companies must engage in responsible lending practices. There are civil and criminal penalties established under the NCCP Act for the provision of unsuitable credit products.

Under the NCCP, Resimac as an Australian Credit Licence (ACL) holder is required to assess the unsuitability of a  credit  product.  The  assessment  process  has  been  established  under  the  NCCP  Act  and  includes  an assessment of the consumer's ability to repay the debt. There are also information verification requirements on all licensees concerning consumers. In effect, the consumer's need and financial capacity will be required to be examined and verified by all licensees under the NCCP Act.

The responsible lending obligations contained in the NCCP Act require all license holders to:

- Make  reasonable  enquiries  about  the  consumers'  financial  situation,  and  their  requirements  and objectives.
- Take reasonable steps to verify the consumers' financial situation.
- Make a preliminary (brokers) or final assessment (lenders) about whether the credit is 'not suitable' for the consumer.

Credit will be unsuitable, if at the time of the assessment it is likely that:

- A consumer will be unable to comply with their financial obligations under the proposed credit contract, or could only comply with substantial hardship; or
- The proposed credit contract will not meet the customer's requirements or objectives.

## 1.5 The Privacy Act 1998

The Privacy Act 1988 (the Act) regulates the use of consumer credit information throughout Australia and also impacts on certain commercial credit related matters. The Act is relevant to all instances where Resimac is requested to seek or divulge information regarding an individual's consumer credit worthiness. In all cases, the  applicant(s)  must  specifically  grant  their  written  consent  beforehand.  Severe  penalties  exist  on  both employee and Resimac for breaches of the Privacy Act.

## 1.6 Anti-Money Laundering &amp; Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (AML / CTF)

Resimac, as a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (AML/CTF), is obliged to undertake certain actions under the legislation's due diligence and additional Know Your Customer (KYC) processes to ensure that adequate measures have been considered to mitigate risks in the acceptance of loan applications. One of these measures is the use of an electronic check for all loan entities (applicants and guarantors, individuals and companies). Electronic checks provide screening of loan applicants for money laundering and terrorist financing activities as can be verified from the databases of the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFAT) and US Treasury Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC).

The electronic check also obtains an electronic verification of the loan applicants from public data sources like the Electoral Roll, Public Phone Number Database, Australia Post's Digital Post al Identifier Database (DPID) and Equifax Verification Database Summary.

As a result, Resimac has implemented the use of the Electronic AML/CTF Check which will be undertaken prior to issuing of an unconditional loan approval and will take effect from 01 June 2010.

Resimac has adopted the services provided by Equifax for their Vericheck AML product.

## 1.8 Communication with Applicant/s

No representation is to be made (verbally or in writing) which would have the effect of committing Resimac to the granting of facilities, extensions or other variations without appropriate credit approval.

Resimac  employees  must  maintain  comprehensive  written  records  of  every  significant  or  material communication (by telephone or otherwise) with or about applicants, guarantors or other intermediaries. Such records must be completed at the time the communication occurs and retained in our systems and on the applicant's file.

Resimac employees are not permitted to provide any legal or financial advice to an applicant(s). Applicant(s) must be advised to seek their own independent professional advice. Responding to general enquiries is not providing professional advice, however any communication is to be limited to general information.

All complaints should be initially directed to the Head of Prime Lending Australia or Head of Specialist Lending Australia. If the matter cannot be satisfactorily resolved, it is to be referred to the General Manager Credit Risk and Asset Management for further action.

## 1.9 Independent Legal &amp; Financial Advice

Independent legal advice (ILA) and financial advice (IFA) is a particular right of the applicant(s), who may elect to decline the right at the time of loan documentation execution.

Circumstances where ILA is a specific condition of the loan include situations where the individual is:

- A guarantor.
- Poor English or non-English speaking background.
- A director / partner that does not have an active role in the business (e.g. a partner with another occupation or home duties).

Or where:

- Doubt or concern exists as to the applicants' ability to understand the obligations under the contract and the consequences.
- Any other situation as determined by Resimac e.g.  National Rental Affordability Scheme (NRAS) property purchase. (Refer Chapter 6.2 -Property Purchase).

Where the applicant or guarantor is heavily dependent on superannuation / annuities or government benefits (e.g. pension, family allowance), it is recommended they obtain independent financial advice to ensure the transaction does not adversely impact their entitlements (i.e. increased assets or income and the potential impact on means / asset testing).

NOTE: Where the loan term exceeds the normal age of retirement (65 years) ILA &amp; IFA may be requested if an  appropriate  exit  strategy  and  serviceability  of  the  loan  (as  determined  by  Resimac)  cannot  be demonstrated  to  the  satisfaction  of  the  Head  of  Prime  Lending  Australia  or  Head  of  Specialist  Lending Australia. (Refer Chapter 11.3 -Undertaking an Assessment).

The Mortgage Originator / Aggregator / Direct Introducer must not nominate the Solicitor or Accountant from whom the applicant(s) seeks legal or financial advice. The applicant(s) must instruct the Solicitor or Accountant and pay fees directly.

An applicant's refusal to obtain ILA and / or IFA where it is a condition of the loan is to be brought to the attention of the Head of Prime Lending Australia or Head of Specialist Lending Australia, who will determine each case on its merits. If not fully satisfied with the reasons for not obtaining ILA and / or IFA, then the loan is to be declined.

It is not mandatory that active directors of a body corporate (company or company trustee) obtain ILA and / or IFA. At the discretion of the appropriate DLA holder however, ILA and / or IFA may be a condition of the loan.

## 1.10 Power of Attorney

A valid reason for the use of an Attorney must be initially established. Once demonstrated, a Power of Attorney (POA) is only accepted where it is granted in favour of a Solicitor and the loan purpose is to the benefit of the applicant(s). Relatives and third parties are not acceptable to reduce the risk of potential fraud and / or duress in executing documentation. A certified copy of the POA will be required on or before settlement.

For Enduring Powers of Attorney signed from 1 April 2004, the Attorney must sign and date a Statement of Acceptance including a statement to avoid acting where there is any conflict of interest between the Donor and Attorney. Where the Attorney is a co-mortgagor or co-transferor the following will also be required:

- Letter  from  the  Donor  or  their  Solicitor  addressed  to  the  Land  &amp;  Property  Information  (LPI)  office confirming that they are aware of and consent to the transaction; or
- Evidence that the transaction is in the interests of the Donor and acceptable to the LPI.

Generally, trustees have no power to delegate their functions. This flows from the principle that a delegate may not further delegate his authority. The Panel Lawyer is to review the POA for all Trust matters prior to application acceptance.

## NOTE:

- *Guarantor(s) are not permitted to sign by way of Power of Attorney.
- **The individual signing under an acceptable Power of Attorney acting as the agent of an applicant must be identified according to Resimac 's Customer Identification procedure.

## 1.11 Panel Solicitors

Resimac  loans  are  to  be  instructed  and  settled  (via  the  Servicer)  using  an  accredited  Panel  Solicitor.  At unconditional stage, data will be electronically provided to both Servicer and the subject Panel Solicitor for documentation preparation within 24 hours.

Panel Solicitors when acting under instruction from Resimac (via the Mortgage Originator) are not permitted to act for any other party to the loan in question (e.g. the applicant).

## Chapter 3 -Applicant Types

Loans are restricted to a single entity and applicant type (i.e. no multiple company applications or blended application types).

## 3. Applicant Types

## 3.1 Individual Applicant

Applicants must be a natural person and be over 18 years of age.

Applicants can be either:

- Australian or New Zealand citizen or hold permanent Australian or New Zealand residency status.
- Companies.
- Discretionary Trusts.

There is no maximum age limit however; the applicant must be able to demonstrate the capacity to repay the loan past the normal age of retirement. In such instances, where the loan will continue to exist after age of retirement, evidence as to how the applicant proposes to repay the loan will be required and must be held on file.

The age of the applicant must therefore be considered to ensure the debt can be repaid within the loan term. Proof of future income must be substantiated at the time of application e.g. superannuation, other assets, or income from their investments.

Refer Chapter 11.3 -Undertaking an Assessment.

## 3.2 Company Applicant

The company must be registered in Australia with the Australian Securities and Investment Commission (ASIC) and be registered for GST where income exceeds the GST threshold of $75,000 per annum.

The directors must be permanent residents of Australia. Directors are to be identified in accordance with AntiMoney Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (AML/CTF) requirements. The details of all shareholders with more than 25% shareholding in the company (if not a director) must be collected.

Company borrowings are to be supported by the unlimited joint and several guarantees of all directors and major shareholders.  Guarantors must be natural persons -guarantees from companies or trusts are not acceptable. A maximum of 4 guarantors is acceptable.

When determining capacity to service the loan, living expenses of the guarantors must be included.

The Mortgage Originator is to hold in the applicant's loan file evidence of the company ACN or ABN. Such details can be obtained from www.search.asic.gov.au

## 3.3 Trust Applicant

There are two scenarios when lending to a Trust:

- A company is acting as trustee for a Trust.

<!-- image -->

- An individual is acting as trustee for a Trust.

All Trust borrowings must be in the name of the trustee(s) as trustee(s) for the Trust.

Where the Trustee is a company, the joint and several guarantees of each of the directors and shareholders of the Trustee company will be required. Resimac policy requirements for corporate applicants apply. (Refer Chapter 3.2 - Company Applicant).

With discretionary Trusts, Resimac does not require the beneficiaries to provide their personal guarantee.

Where the income of a Trustee is required to service the loan then the trustee must also be a beneficiary of the Trust.

In terms of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (AML/CTF), Trust applicants are to be identified in accordance with their structure. It is not necessary to verify the identity of beneficiaries.

Resimac must receive a certified copy of the Trust Deed. The certification must be executed by a Solicitor, Justice of the Peace or Accountant.

NOTE: Resimac will only consider one (1) Trust per loan. The Panel Solicitor will be required to obtain a copy of the Trust Deed and review prior to the settlement of the loan.

## 3.4 Borrower of Convenience / Third Party Securities

A  Borrower  of  Convenience  is  defined  as  a  borrower  that  is  added  to  the  loan  application  to  support serviceability who does not receive a tangible benefit from the loan transaction. These Borrower types are unacceptable.

Third Party securities will not be permitted without evidence that the third party receives a financial benefit from the transaction (Third party is defined as a person (mortgagor) providing security who is not also a borrower).

Borrowers must receive a beneficial interest in the loan transaction by way of joint ownership (50% ownership as a minimum) of the security.

It is accepted that for various reasons (e.g. asset protection, taxation planning) there are times where married / de-facto couples have the ownership of real estate property vested in one party / or one party has less than equal share ownership.

Example: A and B are married. The loan (in A and B's name) is to enable either A or B to purchase a property (in their sole name). Both A and B's income will be taken into account to satisfy the servicing test.

These applications are considered acceptable under the following circumstances:

- Parties must be married or in a de-facto relationship.
- Income of all parties can be taken into account in the servicing test.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

- A Statutory Declaration is to be provided from the borrower not on title to confirm that they hold an equitable interest in the security property and are receiving a benefit in the transaction.

NOTE: Where doubt exists as to the benefit being received by either party, Independent Legal Advice and Financial Advice should be sought (e.g. cash out is sought against the owner-occupied property for business purposes where only one party is a director or shareholder in the business benefiting from the transaction).

Where one party is a guarantor as opposed to a borrower, Independent Legal Advice and Financial Advice is to be sought.

## 3.5 Guarantors

Joint and several guarantees are required from:

- All third-party security providers.
- All directors (particularly shareholder directors) of the borrowing entity (including Trustee Company).
- Any corporate shareholder of the borrowing entity.
- Major shareholders of a borrowing company.

Joint and several guarantees may also be required from other corporate entities controlled by the applicant or any guarantor as determined on a case by case basis (Refer Chapter 11.12 -Company Income).

In  the  case  of  the  applicant  being  a  limited  liability  partnership,  several  guarantees  from  the  individual applicant or unit holders will be required and limited to their respective liability.

Where a guarantee is obtained, a 'clear and direct benefit' must be evident. Resimac will require individual guarantors to receive independent legal and financial advice prior to executing a guarantee. In the case of a third-party guarantee and benefit cannot be readily demonstrated (i.e. a group company situation) an extract of the minutes of the directors meeting where approval is given to providing the guarantee will be required. The minutes must indicate the commercial benefit to the company in it providing the guarantee / security. Commercial benefit may exist in group company situations where the guarantor company may be dependent on the applicant company for financing or ongoing group administration support. In all instances, a commercial benefit to the guarantor company must exist.

Prior to settlement Resimac is to be provided with a Solicitors Certificate confirming advice has been provided. Under  no  circumstances  can  the  guarantee,  disclosure  information  and  supporting  documentation  be provided  to  the  debtor  to  arrange  execution.  All  documentation  must  be  distributed  to  each  guarantor separately.

In  all  cases,  where  there  is  doubt  that  the  guarantor  understands  their  obligations  or  is  uncertain,  the application must not proceed.

In situations where the loan application is joint, all applicants must receive a direct benefit (i.e. any financial and / or material benefit) under the credit contract.

When a 'natural' applicant has insufficient income to service a debt or meet loan to value ratios, an additional person is often made an applicant in order to satisfy income requirements. This additional person is known as a Borrower of Convenience.

<!-- image -->

Therefore,  if  the  third-party signatory is to receive no benefit under the credit contract, but is joined solely for the purpose of being made liable, then the contract is one of guarantee as between the credit provider and the third party.

Applications for Tenants in Common may only be structured as joint applicants in the following situations:

- Applicants are married or de-facto (i.e. applicants must live in the same household); or
- The property share is to be equal i.e. 50:50 for two borrowers.

## 3.6 Unacceptable Applicants

Applications will not be considered from the following:

- Minors under any circumstances.
- Unit Trusts and Hybrid Trusts.

- Existing Bankrupts.
- Applicants under external administration.
- Superannuation  funds  (either  applicant  or  security  provider).  This  excludes  lending  to  Self-Managed Superannuation Funds (SMSF).
- Public companies.
- Owner Builders.
- Non-Resident applicants.
- Limited Liability Companies.
- Associations.
- Churches.
- Clubs.
- Borrowers of Convenience.

## Chapter 5 -Income

## 5. Income

## 5.1 Introduction

Resimac requires applicants to have a proven regular taxable income for serviceability purposes in the form of salary,  wages,  business  earnings,  director's  fees,  trust  distributions,  superannuation  annuity  or  other permanent pension / benefit entitlements.

Acceptable employment types are permanent full time / part time PAYG employment and self-employment.

Some allowances may be considered for income from a second job or for irregular income such as permanent casual  employment,  overtime,  commissions,  bonuses,  shift  allowances  or  investment  income  if  it  can  be substantiated. Allowance may also be given for certain social security benefits such as Family Tax Benefit A&amp;B if  it  is  available  for  a  further  5 years at the time of application. ## Chapter 6 -Loan Purpose

## 6. Loan Purpose

## 6.1 Acceptable Loan Purposes

Loans will be provided to assist with the following:

- Purchase of residential property for either owner occupation or investment purposes.
- Refinance of residential property for either owner occupation or investment purposes.
- Consolidation of debt (e.g. credit cards, personal loans, business loans etc.).
- Home improvements (non-structural only).
- 'Off the Plan' purchase.
- Business Loan.
- Non-real estate investment (shares, managed funds etc.).
- General consumer purposes (e.g. car, holiday etc.).
- Any other purpose deemed worthwhile by Resimac.

## NOTE: Product and LVR restrictions may apply to some of these specific purposes.

All documentation to confirm the purpose of the loan funds must be obtained by the Mortgage Originator and held on each applicant's loan file. This information must be collected to comply with Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (AML/CTF). The evidence of loan purpose documentation will form part of the Resimac audit program.

## 6.2 Property Purchase

For property purchase transactions, a copy of the relevant pages from the signed Contract of Sale is required that evidences the property details and any special conditions, vendor and purchaser names, purchase price, deposit amount and date (within 90 days of any approval). An unsigned contract is acceptable for assessment purposes, however a signed copy must be provided on or prior to settlement.

Where builder / developer rebates or incentives are noted, the value of the rebate or incentive must be discounted from the purchase price. This applies to all builder / developer sales including house and land packages, vacant land, house, home unit, villa or townhouse.

## National Rental Affordability Scheme (NRAS)

Resimac will provide finance on NRAS properties where it has approved the specific NRAS scheme, as well as obtained approval from Genworth LMI that the scheme meets their requirements. Resimac will not entertain funding loans under an NRAS scheme where the ability to take possession of the property is restricted by the NRAS agreement signed by the Investor (purchaser of the security). Arrangements between the Investor and NRAS consortium must not impact the legal relationship between Resimac and the applicant / security.

The following policies apply to NRAS property transactions:

- Prime Loans only.
- NRAS security must be a completed dwelling or a House and Land package.
- Refinancing  of  an  NRAS  property  is  allowed  where  the  refinance  amount  is  dollar  for  dollar  and  no additional funds are borrowed.
- Vacant land purchases, off the plan strata purchases and construction loan purposes are excluded.
- Maximum LVR of 90% plus LMI Capitalisation.
- Maximum of 4 or 25% exposure to Resimac to any individual development, whichever is the lower.
- All  Valuation  Reports  are  to  include  comments  by  the  Valuer  that  the  property  is  a  part  of  NRAS  and comment on any lease terms.
- Serviceability assessment to include the market rental as per valuation report discounted to 65%.
- Tax free incentive payment to be excluded from servicing.
- Application cover sheet to clearly state that the loan security is part of an NRAS Scheme. The application must be submitted for full LMI assessment and approval.
- Independent legal  and  financial  advice  must  be  provided  by  the  applicant(s)  prior  to  settlement.  (This requirement may be waived where the applicant has an existing NRAS Investment Property and proof of NRAS property ownership is held on file).

Resimac will accept loan applications for the purchase of NRAS properties subject to Resimac 's NRAS policy guidelines where the properties are managed / administered by one of the following consortiums:

- Yaran Residential Investments Pty Ltd.

## 6.4 Home Improvements

Intended renovations and improvements that do not substantially affect the structural integrity of the security property  (e.g.  room,  patio,  garage  extensions,  and  kitchen  or  bathroom  renovations)  would  constitute  a cosmetic renovation and may be released to the applicant(s) without supervision, subject to:

- Where the value of the home improvements exceeds $50,000 the applicant(s) must provide a signed letter detailing the work to be undertaken and / or a registered licensed builder's quotation as to cost.
- For home improvements of $50,000 or less where proposed changes are non-structural, Introducer notes advising what works are to be completed are acceptable.
- The 'As Is' value of the property being adopted for LVR purposes.

NOTE: Where there is a major structural change proposed to the property (e.g. roof being replaced or second storey addition) these are unacceptable to Resimac.

## 6.5 'Off the Plan' Purchase

Applications for ' off the p lan' purchase may be considered subject to the following:

- Where any purchase is 'off the plan' or out of date, a full copy of the Contract is required.
- Refers  to  unit  /  townhouse  developments  only  whereby  a  property  is  purchased  'off  the  plan'  and settlement of the property is 12 months (or more).
- An updated valuation report is obtained prior to settlement of the loan confirming that the property is now complete.
- The valuation report provides comparable sales of similar properties outside the new development.
- If the 'On Completion' value is higher than the original purchase price and the Contract of Sale was signed more than 12 months ago, Resimac will rely on the valuation. However, the loan amount must remain the same as the original amount approved and the loan amount cannot exceed the original purchase price.
- If the 'O n Completion ' value is lower than the purchase price, then Resimac will rely upon the valuation amount for LVR purposes.

<!-- image -->

- Where the vendor of the purchase is a large corporate property developer who is selling the property directly or via a related entity (acting as the Estate Agent) i.e. sale is not through a 3 rd party Real Estate agent, and it is identified that the purchaser has a direct association with the vendor e.g. architect, builder, employee of the developer etc. a DLA6 approval sign off is required.
- Where the vendor is not considered a large corporate property developer the transaction must be at arm's length in all instances.

## 6.6 Business Loan

In addition to the standard loan parameters, the business purpose loan is guided by the following examples:

- Refinance of business borrowings such as bank term loans, bill facilities and overdrafts.
- Purchase of a business (e.g. newsagency, taxi license, franchise etc.).
- Purchase non-residential real estate other than the security property (e.g. commercial property).
- Purchase of plant and equipment.

In addition, business loans must satisfy the following:

- Maximum loan portion $500,000.
- Maximum LVR 85%.
- For any loan with a business purpose loan portion the maximum LVR for the total loan is 85%.
- Where the total loan is greater than $500,000, the maximum loan amount for the business component is limited to $500,000.
- Projected  income shall  not  be  used  (but  losses  from  any  existing  business  being  purchased  should  be considered).
- Preferably applicants will be self-employed in the same business for the past 2 years.
- Start-up of a new business will not be considered.

## 6.7 Cash Out

Under the Know Your Customer (KYC) policy of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (AML/CTF) legislation, it is a requirement to obtain information substantiating the purpose of the loan and it must be clearly noted on the application.

Any loan or component of a loan that is to be used for anything other than for the purchase of a residential property or refinance / consolidation of a current mortgage / loan(s) where proceeds are being either fully or in part released directly to the applicant will be classified as cash out.

- Up to 85% LVR there is no cash out limit.
- 85.01 up to 90% cash out component is limited to 20% of the security value.

Variances will be considered at the discretion of Resimac provided:

- There is supporting documentation evidencing use of funds or
- Resimac is to exercise control over the release of funds.

Examples of acceptable supporting documentation are:

- Builder's quote.
- Purchase Contract.
- Confirmation from a Financial Planner or Accountant as to the intended use of funds.

## NOTE:

- Applications where cash out is being sought to purchase a property where its best use and / or value is derived from development and re-sale, even if said property is not being offered / taken as security, is considered unacceptable.
- Where cash out is for business use or business debt (currently secured elsewhere) is being refinanced against the applicants Owner-Occupied property or sole significant asset the Underwriter is to ensure the proportion of debt being sought / refinanced is in line with the ownership of the business and all borrowers are obtaining benefit from the transaction. If financial benefit is not evident the party not obtaining a benefit should be considered as a Guarantor (Refer Chapter 3.4 Borrower of Convenience / Third Party Securities and 3.5 Guarantors).

## 6.8 Advantageous / Favourable Purchase (between related parties including landlord / tenant)

This refers to purchases at below current market value. Resimac will consider such an application provided the following factors are evident:

- The loan purpose is restricted to the purchase and associated costs i.e. no cash out, refinancing / debt consolidating etc.
- The Valuer must be advised that it is an advantageous purchase and note the purchase price and the nature of the transaction in the subject report.
- All circumstances must be fully substantiated to validate the discount rationale (i.e. copy of applicable birth certificate(s) to confirm family association, letter from landlord confirming landlord-tenant relationship).
- A copy of the title transfer is obtained at settlement to confirm correct stamp duty has been paid on the purchase price.
- For favourable purchase, the LVR is calculated by dividing the loan amount by the valuation amount.
- The applicant must be able to demonstrate a minimum 5% of the purchase price from genuine savings where the LVR is in excess of 90% (where genuine savings is not evident and LVR is in excess of 90%, the loan is to be treated under Quickstart parameters).

## 6.9 NonArm's Length Transaction (between non -related parties)

This refers to nonarm's length transaction where the sale of property does not involve a registered Real Estate Agent acting for the vendor (consider any conflicts of interest on the Contract e.g. same solicitor acting for vendor and purchaser) and the parties are NOT related.

This also refers to transactions where a vendor is selling the property at a discounted price to a person to whom he / she is indebted.

Resimac will consider such an application provided the following factors are evident:

- The Valuer must be advised that the purchase is without the intervention of a registered Real Estate Agent and note the purchase price and the nature of the transaction in the subject report.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

- A copy of the title transfer is obtained at settlement to confirm correct stamp duty has been paid on the purchase price.
- For nonarm's length transactions, the LVR is calculated by dividing the loan amount by the lower of the Contract of Sale or the valuation amount.
- Borrowings are to be limited to 95% of the purchase price or valuation whichever is the lesser. The 95% LVR is inclusive of any capitalised LMI fee.
- The applicant must be able to demonstrate the minimum genuine savings contribution.

NOTE: If doubt exists, the file must be referred to a Quality Assurance Officer for review.

## 6.10 Unacceptable Loan Purposes

Resimac prohibits the use of loan funds to finance the following but are not limited to:

- Loans secured by non-residential property.
- Consumer Credit Insurance (i.e. Mortgage Protection Insurance or insurance covering the capacity of the applicant to make repayments under the loan contract due to sickness, injury or disability, unemployment etc.) not issued through Resimac. This is distinct from LMI.
- Loans  for  development  finance  including  applications  where  cash  out  is  being  sought  to  purchase  a property where its best use and / or value is derived from development and re-sale, even if said property is not being offered / taken as security.
- Bridging loans.
- Construction loans.
- Vendor finance structures.
- Purchase or expansion of a business outside the core business expertise of the  applicant(s) and where management track record is not evident.
- Acquisition of security properties for immediate sale via a term contract.
- Purchase of transportable / mobile homes and prefabricated / kit homes.
- Second mortgage security.
- Payment of taxation liabilities.
- Refinancing of loans that are currently or have been in arrears.

In addition, facilities that are to be used for an illegal activity or for purposes not in keeping with Resimac 's standing are specifically excluded. Such facilities include, but are not limited to, providing finance for:

- Assistance to a company to enable a person to acquire shares in that company unless that transaction is fully compliant with the requirements of the Corporations Act 2001 and ASIC Act 2001.
- Illegal arms or drug dealing.
- Illegal / unauthorised gambling enterprises.

## Chapter 7 -Security Types

## 7. Security Types

## 7.1 Introduction

Resimac requires a first ranking registrable mortgage. The mortgage is to be registered under the appropriate statute of the State or Territory in which the property is located. The mortgage must also be fully amortising and secure only one loan (no cross collateralisation of loans i.e. each loan is 'stand alone' in that it has separate security (noting there can be multiple securities and multiple splits under that loan)).

Care is to be taken where at the time of initial underwriting it is known that a second mortgage / caveat is to be lodged that specifically relates to funding required to complete the transaction. A proposed 2 nd mortgage / caveat at the time of initial underwriting typically indicates a shortfall of funds to complete the transaction, necessitating  additional  borrowings  that  should  be  factored  into  the  servicing  test  and  overall  credit assessment.  2 nd mortgages  to  accommodate  employment  entitlements  /  benefits  (e.g.  Defence  Housing Schemes) are considered acceptable where serviceability of the total debt is evidenced.

Lodgement of other instrument types (e.g. lease) where there is no specific loan / funding facility being entered into in order to complete the transaction is considered acceptable.

Security  must  be  taken  as  a  safeguard  to  protect  Resimac  against  contingencies  which  may  prevent applicants(s) from fulfilling their repayment obligations. Specific requirements must be determined on a case by case basis with appropriate minimum standards established.  As the final mechanism for debt repayment, Resimac will not compromise on the standard required for acceptable security properties in terms of overall liquidity and concentration risks.

## 8.1 Introduction

In essence, a valuation is an independent check. A formal valuation serves as an independent opinion of value, provides information that helps assess whether the asset is suitable security (e.g. highlight environmental issues, restrictive covenants on title etc.) and provides information that may indicate that the value of the asset is dependent on certain events (e.g. present tenancy arrangements, rezoning approvals etc.). Formal asset values adopted must reflect the realisable value at valuation date based on historical evidence available.

An acceptable valuation is required in all cases to confirm Resimac 's security position.

Only an accredited Panel Valuer of Resimac, can value properties as security for a loan. (Refer Appendix 1).

## 8.2 Minimum Valuer Qualification/s

## Properties Valued at $2 Million Or Less

Each valuation must have been completed by a Valuer in one of the following membership categories or the equivalent membership category of an equivalent professional body:

- AAPI CPV (Associate Member of the API Certified Practising Valuer)
- FAPI CPV (Fellow Member of the API Certified Practising Valuer)
- PMAPI RPV (Provisional Member of the API Residential Property Valuer)
- LFAPI CPV (Life Member of the API Certified Practising Valuer)

Valuation  reports  completed  by  PMAPI  RPV  members  are  only  acceptable  where  countersigned  by  a supervising Member holding AAPI CPV, FAPI CPV of LFAPI CPV classification. The supervising member must review the valuation and working papers and based upon such review and appropriate questioning obtain reasonable  satisfaction  that  the  value  opinion  contained  in  the  valuation  has  been  reached,  based  on reasonable grounds.

## Properties Valued at More Than $2 Million

Each valuation must be completed by a Valuer in either the AAPI CPV, FAPI CPV or LFAPI CPV membership category or an equivalent membership category of an equivalent professional body. Further, each valuation completed by an AAPI CPV must be countersigned by either an AAPI CPV, FAPI CPV or LFAPI CPV who is a director or principal of the relevant valuation entity.

Valuations  on  properties  valued  at  more  than  $2  million  cannot  be  undertaken  by  a  PMAPI  RPV  or  an equivalent membership category of an equivalent professional body.

## 8.5 Independent Valuation Report (Short or Full)

An Independent Valuation Report (Short or Full) from an approved Panel Valuer must be obtained in respect of each property securing a Resimac loan.  Resimac will employ the following minimum valuation standards.

- Comply with Resimac 's criteria.
- Each valuation submitted must be in the API PropertyPro template format and include the 4-property related and 4 market related risk ratings.
- The valuation must be addressed to the Mortgage Originator and extended for the benefit and use of Resimac Limited and Resimac 's approved LMI provider .
- The valuation report is to be extended for use by the Security Trustee.
- The valuation report must be for mortgagee purposes.
- The valuation report must be no more than 3 months old at date of approval and not more than 3 months old at time of settlement.
- The original valuation report should contain a minimum of 5 colour photographs with views of the front / rear of the property, kitchen, bathroom and any view, significant external improvements or defects of the property (e.g. harbour view, swimming pool, structural defects to the improvements etc.).  Panel Valuers can submit valuation reports either in hardcopy or, preferably as an electronic file (secured PDF with the signature of the Valuer).
- The photographs should be date and time stamped and if this function is not available the Valuer should provide  comments  (under  Additional  Comments)  within  the  report  setting  out  the  date  and  time  the photographs were taken. If the Valuer cannot comply with the above minimum requirements, the report must  provide  specific  comments  regarding  the  reason  for  not  complying  with  the  photographic requirements and confirmation that the Valuer has fully inspected the property (in line with valuation industry expectations).
- The basis of the valuation for lending purposes will be an 'As Is'  current  market value  and should be considered saleable with a selling period of 3 but no more than 6 months.
- The  valuation  report  must  contain  comparable  sales  evidence  no  older  than  180  days  on  a  vacant possession basis which should be 'like for like' i.e. if valuing a 2-bedroom house the comparable must be for a similar properties located in the immediate suburb or surrounding areas.
- Valuation reports remain the property of Resimac.

To eliminate the risk of fraud, a loan application is not unconditionally approved until the original valuation report is received directly from the Panel Valuer.

## NOTE:

- Where comparable sales provided are within 180 days, however are not considered to be relative or within preferred tolerance (+/ -15%), of a different class of property or sale in a substantially different location is relied  upon,  these  are  considered  acceptable  provided  appropriate  comments  supporting  their  use  is provided by the Valuer.
- Where all comparable sales are older than 180 days, acceptance of the valuation will need to be considered as a security property variance.
- Where  the  valuation  report  notes  that  the  security  unit  /  apartment  is  located  within  a  building  / development appearing to be or is known to be clad, building / construction expert (e.g. engineer, builder, building  surveyor,  certifier  etc.)  confirmation  is  required  to  determine  whether  cladding  is  compliant / conforming with Laws and Codes.
- If the valuation has expired at the time of settlement Resimac will require either a letter from the Valuer confirming  original  valuation  amount  or  a  new  valuation  based  on  the  requirements  of  the  Valuers Professional Indemnity Insurance policy.

## 8.10 Home Building Insurance

At the time of settlement, the relevant property must be insured under a Home Building Insurance Policy in the name(s) of the applicant(s) with the Trustee registered as the party of interest.

## 8.11 Breach of Representation

In the event that the valuation report provided to Resimac in support of an application is not true in whole or in part, the LMI (if applicable) may:

- a) Avoid the relevant LMI policy if the misrepresentation was made fraudulently.
- b) Reduce its liability under the relevant LMI policy to the amount that would place the LMI in the position in which the LMI would have been if the misrepresentation had not been made; and or
- c) Cancel the relevant LMI policy.

## 9. Genuine Savings / Deposits / Equity

In all cases, the source of the applicant 's equity in the proposed transaction must be established.

## Full Doc

Applicants  must  demonstrate  genuine  savings  of  at  least  5%  of  the  proposed  purchase  price,  held  or accumulated over a minimum period of 3 months where the LVR is &gt; 90%.

Genuine savings can be from:

## Chapter 11 -Credit Assessments

## 11. Credit Assessments

## 11.1 Lending Principles

Resimac is prepared to consider the provision of financial accommodation to an applicant where capacity to repay  over  an  acceptable  term  can  be  demonstrated,  acceptable  security  is  available  and  a  satisfactory explanation is provided behind the request for credit.

Subsequently, each Resimac loan application must satisfy the following components:

- Capacity to Service.
- Acceptable Security.
- Satisfactory explanation as to the applicant 's request for credit.

## 11.2 Responsible Lending Under the NCCP Act

Resimac  as  an  ACL  holder  is  required  to  adhere  to  a  range  of  specific  conduct  obligations  relevant  to responsible  lending.  The  responsible  lending  conduct  obligations  set  in  place  the  expected  standards  of behaviour for those engaging in credit activities with consumers.

The objective of the responsible lending obligations is to ensure that an ACL holder does not:

- Enter into a credit contract with an applicant;
- Suggest a credit contract to an applicant; or
- Assist an applicant to apply for a new credit contract or an increase in an existing credit contract

if the credit contract is ' unsuitable ' .

The responsible lending obligations for ACL holders under the NCCP Act require:

- Making reasonable inquiries about the applicant's financial situation, requirements and objective;
- Taking reasonable steps to verify the applicant's financial situation; and
- Making a final assessment as to whether the credit contract is 'not unsuitable' for the applicant ba sed on the information from the first two steps.

## 11.3 Undertaking an Assessment

The primary test of credit worthiness is one of client capacity and willingness to meet repayment obligations. The underlying principal in determining what is acceptable evidence of income for the purpose of assessing an applicant's capacity to meet proposed loan repayments, is the need to establish the authenticity of the income and that it is regular and continuing. The applicant(s) is to disclose and provide documentary evidence of all sources of income which they require to be included for serviceability purposes.

Only income which is able to be fully substantiated can be included in the serviceability calculation.

Specific tests have been designed to ensure that the applicant's overall financial condition is acceptable and adequate to cover all commitments with allowance for variables including interest rate movements. As a

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

minimum, appropriate financial analysis will be required in relation  to  the  applicant  /  primary  repayment source.

We can never guarantee that an applicant's income will continue at their current level however, where we can show a pattern of income over a reasonable time period then the risk of any adverse change in current income level is reduced.

Reasonable enquiries must be made about an applicant's financial situation so as to determine their ability to meet all the repayments, fees, charges and transaction costs associated with the mortgage contract.

## These include:

- The applicant(s) current source of income (full time, part time, casual etc.).
- The applicant(s) fixed living expense amounts.
- The applicant(s) discretionary living expense amounts e.g. private school fees, additional motor vehicle expenses, Pay TV, mobile phone expenses, gym or other memberships (where declared).
- The extent to which any existing credit contracts are to be repaid, in full or in part from the mortgage advanced.
- The applicant(s) credit history, including any existing or previous defaults.
- The applicant(s) future prospects inclu ding any significant change in the applicant(s)' financial circumstances that is reasonably foreseeable.

Resimac must undertake a 'final' assessment that a credit contract is 'not unsuitable' based on the applicant's financial situation, requirements and objectives before entering into a mortgage contract. It is imperative that the credit staff record the actions undertaken in forming their assessments. Credit staff should consider two main questions when assessing a loan application.

1. Will the applicant be able to meet their financial obligations without substantial financial hardship? and
2. Does the mortgage contract meet the applicant's requirements and objectives?

If the applicant can only repay by selling the applicant's principal place of residence, it is presumed the loan will cause financial hardship unless the contrary is proved. This presumption relates only to the applicant's principal  place  of  residence.  This  presumption  will  not  apply  if  the  applicant  has  to  sell  other  assets,  for example  an  investment  property. However ,  relying  on  sale  of  assets  needs  to  be  reasonable  in  all circumstances of the case.

The  assessment  should  be  based  on  the  information  to  hand  as  a  result  of  the  inquiries  and  verification completed.  The  outcome  of  the  assessment  is  t o  ascertain  that  the  proposed  mortgage  contract  is  'not unsuitable' for the applicant(s).

## 11.4 Exit Strategies / Mature Aged Applicants / Loan Term Expiry

There is no maximum age limit above which we will not lend, however either:

- an exit strategy must be demonstrated; or
- the applicant must be able to demonstrate the capacity to repay the loan past the normal age of retirement.

In assessing the suitability of the applicant's exit strategy, consideration is to be taken with regards to the applicant's industr y where income is derived and the overall financial position of the applicant including what

<!-- image -->

substantial saleable assets are held. Confirmation of any significant saleable asset(s) must be obtained and held on file where any doubt exists e.g. share account balance statements, confirmation of ownership of any other properties and corresponding debts, superannuation balance statements etc.

Where the  loan  term  exceeds  the  normal  age  of  retirement  and the  security  property  is  the  applicant's principal place of residence, or significant tangible and verifiable asset, a detailed written confirmation from the  applicant's  outlining  their  proposed  exit  strategy  must  be  supplied  and  found  satisfactory  to  the Underwriter.

If  the applicant can only repay by selling their principal place of residence or their significant tangible and verifiable asset, it is presumed the loan will cause financial hardship unless the contrary is proved.

In  instances  where  an  exit  strategy  involves  downsizing  of  the  applicant's  principle  pl ace  of  residence  at retirement in order to repay the loan and / or to release equity to supplement retirement income, then careful consideration should be given. In addition to a written detailed exit strategy that addresses what the applicant is  expecting to occur (e.g. expected sale price, timeframe involved and funds remaining etc.) Independent Legal Advice &amp; Independent Financial Advice may be requested if any doubt exists to the appropriateness of the documented exit strategy provided and / or serviceability of the loan past the normal age of retirement (as determined by Resimac).

Where the Underwriter considers the written exit strategy by the applicant unacceptable, the loan term is to be reduced within normal retirement age (consider applicant employment industry).

Possible future inheritances are not considered an acceptable exit strategy.

NOTE: The exit strategy must come directly from the applicants e.g. via a signed and dated letter or a direct email  from  the  applicants  (provided  it  can  easily  be  identified  that  the  email  has  originated  from  the applicants i.e. email address, email date &amp; time, email header is evident).

## 11.7 Capacity to Service

Each credit must demonstrate that the applicant(s) has the ability to service and repay the facility through primary and secondary sources.

The primary source of repayment will generally relate to either, or a combination of:

- The applicant 's normal business, personal exertion income / cash flow and / or net rental.
- The facility purpose (e.g. rental / income flow from an investment).

The secondary sources of repayment generally relate to the sale of the assets and are secondary insofar as they provide a fall-back position should the primary source fail.

Resimac will not approve a credit where our analysis shows it will be necessary to rely on security as a principal source of repayment.

Resimac utilises a Net Surplus Ratio (NSR) calculation in determining an applicant 's  capacity to repay. The Resimac serviceability calculator is to be completed for each application.

## Net Surplus Ratio

The NSR calculation is net income after tax less living expenses of all applicants and guarantors, divided by the amount of the total financial commitments including the new loan repayment.

## Example

Total net annual income after tax less living expenses:

$55,000

Total financial expenses:

$30,000

NSR = 1.83 times ($55,000 / $30,000)

The minimum NSR limit is 1.00 times (with a required minimum 'Surplus Cash after Financial Expenses' of $200).

## 11.8 Assessment Interest Rates

For  serviceability  calculations  the  proposed  borrowings  (including  any  capitalised  fees)  are  to  be assessed using the Resimac assessment interest rate which will be the higher of the actual applicant rate plus a 3.00% loading, or the floor assessment rate of 5.75%.

This provides Resimac with a level of comfort that the applicant can still service their loan without financial duress in the event of interest rate rises and unforeseen expenses.

<!-- image -->

All loans are to be assessed on Principal &amp; Interest over a maximum term of 30 years (or term of loan if less), except where an Interest Only period has been provided (or a Line of Credit term) the assessment of the applicants ability to make Principal &amp; Interest repayments should be over the remainder of the loan term, and not the whole loan term i.e. if a 30 year term loan has a 5 year Interest Only period, serviceability should be assessed on repaying Principal &amp; Interest over 25 years (not 30).

The Resimac assessment rate used for the loan being applied for, is also to apply to all existing Resimac debts and other existing non Resimac mortgage debts.

For existing QBE insured loans the QBE 'benchmark rate' is to apply .

## 11.9 Estimated Living Expenses

Resimac 's living expenses are based on:

- The higher of the applicant(s) declared living expenses or the  Resimac Living Expense Index (being the Henderson Poverty Index plus a margin of 15% calculated automatically on single adults, adult couples and dependent children).
- Where it is deemed the living expenses advised by the applicant are unreasonable i.e. $50 per month for food (groceries, take away) these items are to be investigated for accuracy and / or substituted with a reasonable expense figure where one can be determined.

When the applicant is a Company or a Trust, the living expenses to be applied are the higher of the directors (who provide their guarantee) declared living expenses or the Resimac Living Expense Index.

## 11.10 Notional Rent

Where the applicant(s) is purchasing / refinancing an investment property and is said to reside with family or friends either rent free or at an unusually low cost, Resimac will apply a rental living expense of $150 per week ($650 per month) per applicant as a commitment.

NOTE: This is not intended to apply to applicants with living quarters provided as part of their employment with equity contribution evident and verified.

## 11.11 Personal Credit Card Assessment

An assessment rate of 3.8% of the authorised credit card limit is to be adopted for servicing purposes.

## 11.12 Buy Now Pay Later Facility

Where a 'buy now pay later' facility is disclosed on the Assets and Liabilities, within the living expense or where the bank statements held show expense(s) relating to a 'buy now pay later' financing platform (e.g. Afterpay, Zip Money, Openpay, Humm, Payright, Klarna etc.) the higher of the limit, balance or original loan amount is to be captured as a credit card liability for servicing purposes i.e. assessed at 3.8% of the higher of the limit, balance or original loan amount.

NOTE: Where  the  facility  is  being  refinanced  or  consolidated,  refer  Chapter  6.3  Refinance  &amp;  Debt Consolidation for requirements.

## 11.13 Company Income

The Net Profit before Tax (NPBT) must be entered into the calculator under 'Company income'. The calculator will  deduct  company  tax  from  the  NPBT  amount  to  calculate  a  Net  Profit  after  Tax  (NPAT)  amount  for serviceability. The Company income fields are only to be used where the Company is both the income source and the borrower. In all other circumstances retained profit etc. must be added to gross taxable individual income.

Where  there  is  a  significant  reliance  on  the  yearly  retained  profits  from  a  related  company  then  an acknowledgement from the Company should be obtained and a guarantee provided by the Company.

The acknowledgement to be provided is to read as follows:

'I hereby acknowledge that the ability of (Insert Borrower(s) name) to service the proposed commitment is dependent on increased salary/wages/drawings/dividends to be derived in the future from (Insert Company name) . '

The Acknowledgement is to be signed by all Directors / Shareholders of the related Company.

The Acknowledgement, along with the need to obtain a Guarantee, from the related Company is to be included in the Formal Approval as a Special Condition to be satisfied prior to settlement.

## 11.14 Investment Loans &amp; Negative Gearing

Resimac allows Negative Gearing taxation benefits when calculating loan serviceability on investment property purpose loans at the actual rate of the loan facility being sought. Negative gearing on other non-property asset types e.g. shares is not acceptable.

## 11.15 Taxation

The current Individual Resident Taxation rates are built into the Resimac Serviceability Calculator.

## 11.16 Joint Income / Joint Commitments

For servicing purposes, Resimac will adopt the following:

- Where the applicants  have  existing  joint  commitments  with  parties  who  are  not  included  in  the  loan application, 100% of the existing commitment is to be used in calculating serviceability for the new loan.
- If  the applicants share a positive income source such as rental income with parties not included in the subject transaction, the applicant's tax return or certificate of title is to be used to ascertain the percentage of ownership. The percentage of ownership will then be applied to the gross joint income to determine the amount used in calculating serviceability for the new loan.

## 11.17 Single Applicant with Non-Applicant Spouse

For a married person who is the sole applicant on a loan, notional living costs for a couple will be applied unless independent income from the non-applicant spouse can be evidenced by the most recent payslip.

## 12. Credit Decision

## 12.1 Introduction

Each credit application will be carefully assessed by the appropriate delegated officer.  Once the assessment is complete the officer will:

- Approve, subject to any additional conditions as required, or decline applications which fall within their delegated lending authority.
- Make a recommendation, which may include  additional  conditions,  to  approve  applications  which  fall outside their delegated lending authority.

No annual reviews will be undertaken of obligors.

A Conditional credit approval will be valid for up to 30 days from the date on which it is given.

An Unconditional credit approval will be valid for up to 90 days from the date on which it is given.

NOTE: If  the approval has expired at the time of settlement, Resimac will require a signed letter from the applicant(s) confirming the original loan application details have not changed i.e. employment and income details, liabilities, number of dependents etc. Should the applicant's loan application details change, the loan must be re-assessed to confirm adherence to Resimac 's policies and suitability of the loan through NCCP prior to settlement.

## 12.2 Policy Exceptions

Many  criteria  in  the  Lending  Procedures  and  Underwriting  Guidelines  originate  from  the  Credit  Risk Management Credit Policy document which every DLA holder must review and fully understand.

The approval of any loan that is outside the criteria detailed in the Credit Risk Management Credit Policy is defined as a policy exception. Policy exceptions can only be approved by appropriate DLA holders (i.e. where sign off by 2 Management signatures or the Credit Committee has been provided) where:

- The additional credit risks have been satisfactorily mitigated and
- The ability to securitise the relevant loan will not be adversely impacted.

Where an exception approval authority is exercised, the reasons for approval (including concise details of specific risk mitigants in respect of the areas of non-compliance) must be recorded on the application under the approving officer's signature within RUS.

All exception approvals must be noted in RUS for appropriate monitoring and monthly reporting purposes. It is the responsibility of the Underwriter to ensure that the exception is fully completed and signed off by an authorised DLA holder. Refer Chapter 12 - Credit Decision for additional information.

The approval of loans outside the Lending Procedures and Underwriting Guidelines Manual (i.e. those not contained in the Credit Risk Management Credit Policy) are defined as variances. A variance can be approved by an appropriately authorised DLA holder.

## 12.6 Appeals Against Decisions

In some instances there may be valid grounds for raising an appeal against a credit decision.

The appeal procedure is outlined as follows:

## First Stage

Where grounds for disputing a decision relate to new information, such information is to be provided that will explain the matter fully to the Underwriter, and request that matter be reconsidered.

Where there remains dissent as to whether the proposal should proceed, the proposal should be submitted to the next highest authority. In doing so, it will be made clear that the proposal does not carry the support of the person whose decision is being appealed.

## Second Stage

## Chapter 13 -Lenders Mortgage Insurance

## 13. Lenders Mortgage Insurance

All Prime Insured loans must be 100% mortgage insured.

The approved mortgage insurers to the Resimac Prime Insured Mortgage Programme are:

- Genworth Financial Mortgage Insurance Pty Ltd and
- QBE Lender's Mortgage Insurance Limited.

The insured party covered by LMI is the Lender. For Resimac loans this is 'The Trustee' and not the applicant.

The applicant has no rights under the policy and is not covered in the event of default.

LMI insures the Trustee in the event the applicant defaults on the mortgage repayments. That is, should the security property be sold and a shortfall occurs, the Trustee can recover this shortfall under the LMI policy.

The mortgage insurer has the right to recover any claim paid to the Trustee from the borrower. Although the mortgage may have been released from the property, the borrower(s) are not released from the outstanding debt, and this debt becomes liable to the mortgage insurer.

Resimac or the Mortgage Originator when submitting the loan proposal to the mortgage insurer has a 'Duty of Disclosure' under the Insurance Act to confirm all the information is correct.

A single mortgage insurer must insure every Resimac loan. When there is more than one security to the loan, the loan may not be split between mortgage insurers.

Any loan which has been previously submitted to a mortgage insurer and has been declined or which has been submitted to, but rejected, by another securitiser, must be separately advised to the mortgage insurer and Resimac by the Mortgage Originator. Such loans shall not be included in the pool without the prior approval of both Resimac and the mortgage insurer.

## Chapter 14 -Quality Assurance

## 14. Quality Assurance

## 14.1 Introduction

The Underwriting Quality Assurance (QA) programme is a Credit Risk and Asset Management function within Resimac developed to provide assurance to Management and the Board that loan applications are assessed in compliance with Resimac Credit Risk Management Lending Policies and Procedures. The programme primarily aims to identify systemic issues with the lending approval process.

The main objectives of the programme are to:

- Identify training needs of Resimac Lending staff and other Resimac staff.
- Provide assurance that loan applications are compliant with credit legislation e.g. the National Consumer Credit Protection Act (NCCP), Privacy Act and Anti-Money Laundering &amp; Counter Financing of Terrorism Act (AML/CFT).
- Ensure the latest version of the Resimac Credit Risk Management Procedures and Underwriting Guidelines; LMI providers Underwriting Policies and servicing calculators etc. are being used.
- Determine whether decision makers are making their decision within their Delegated Lending Authorities (DLA) or have escalated the decision to a higher authority if needed.
- Identify whether the decision to approve an application has been justified as needed.
- Detect  instances  of  misleading  information  or  fraud  and  prevent  further  risk  of  loss  to  Resimac  by prohibiting the loan from progressing any further.
- To  communicate  the  results  of  the  QA  programme  to  the  General  Manager  Credit  Risk  and  Asset Management  and  Head  of  Prime  Lending  Australia  or  Head  of  Specialist  Lending  Australia.  Give recommendations in relation to lending policy and procedures, product criteria and Introducer reviews.
- Provide reporting to the General Manager Credit Risk and Asset Management as required.

## 14.2 Roles &amp; Responsibilities / Authorities

The Underwriting QA programme is managed by Credit Risk and Asset Management.

The process of implementing the QA programme is the responsibility of the following parties who have been given authority to act.

## Quality Assurance Officer

Is responsible for:

- Review selected files in accordance with the scope outlined in Chapter 14.5.

## Manager Credit Administration

Is responsible for:

- Providing monthly QA review reports.
- Ensuring that the Resimac Credit Risk Management Procedures and Underwriting Guidelines Manual is accurate and current.
- Ensuring delegated lending staffs operate within their delegation.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

- In conjunction with the Head of Prime Lending Australia or Head of Specialist Lending Australia, monitor DLA holders QA results to determine whether action needs to be taken e.g. revoking an Underwriter's delegation or reducing an Underwriter's delegation.

## Head of Prime Lending Australia / Head of Specialist Lending Australia

Is responsible for:

- Implementing staff training as required to consistently achieve satisfactory QA review results.
- Rectifying errors identified through the QA programme in order to ensure all loan applications are fully compliant with Resimac 's Credit Risk M anagement Procedures and Underwriting Guidelines.
- In  conjunction  with  the  General  Manager  Credit  Risk and Asset  Management monitor DLA holders QA results  to  determine  whether  action  needs  to  be  taken  e.g.  revoking  an  Underwriter's  delegation  or reducing an Underwriter's delegation.

## 14.3 Rationale

There are a number of reasons why the QA programme has been adopted.

- The programme offers real time review of the applications and decision makers.
- The programme allows Resimac to decline an application should the QA review uncover non-compliance with the Resimac Credit Risk Management Procedures and Underwriting Guidelines Manual which may warrant overturning the original decision.
- The QA programme is a Risk Management function implemented to mitigate the risk of future financial loss arising from the assessment and acceptance of poor quality loans.

## 14.4 Approach

The QA reviews are conducted on unconditionally approved new applications on a daily basis.

- The number of unconditional application files to be reviewed under the QA programme will be at the discretion of the General Manager Credit Risk and Asset Management.
- The minimum number of applications reviewed under QA is not to be less than 10% of the total number of unconditional approvals per month.

## 14.5 Scope

Selected application files will be reviewed and assessed on the basis of their compliance with the latest version of  Resimac 's Credit  Risk  Management  Procedures  and  Underwriting  Guidelines  Manual  in  relation  to  the following areas:

- Applicant(s) / Guarantor(s) -are they acceptable?
- AML/CTF Identification -Completed ID form and ID's held on file and are acceptable?
- NCCP compliance -has  the  loan  been  assessed  as  being  'not  unsuitable'  and  an  exit  strategy exists  if needed?
- Loan Purpose -is the loan purpose(s) acceptable and within guidelines i.e. cash out?
- Equifax -have the enquiries been properly investigated and are all the related reports held?
- Employment -has it been verified for both PAYE and Self-Employed applicants (if required)?

<!-- image -->

- Income -is proof of income held, searches completed correctly and Resimac verification form(s) completed if required?
- Capacity -has a correct calculator been used and has NSR been properly calculated?
- Equity -do the applicants have genuine savings and / or proof of funds to complete evident?
- Refinance -is there the minimum required refinance statements held on file? Is a Rates Notice held?
- Valuations -is the security acceptable and are photos held with the report?
- LVR -is it within guidelines?
- Delegated Lending Authority -has the loan been approved by a correct DLA holder?
- Variance / Exception -has the variance / exception been justified and approved by a correct DLA holder?

A 'Quality Assurance Review' Excel spread sheet is completed with a 'Yes' or 'No' answer to the above questions with comments inserted as appropriate. Based on the information recorded on the spread sheet the reviewer will use their judgement and experience to make a determination as to whether or not the loan was assessed correctly with the decision justified. Application files considered to have failed the review using the  above criteria  will  be  communicated  to  the  Manager Underwriting Prime Lending and  Head of  Prime Lending  Australia  or  Head  of  Specialist  Lending  Australia  outlining  the  reasons  why  the  application  was considered not acceptable.

## 14.6 Grading

Applications reviewed under the QA Programme will be categorised as follows:

- Satisfactory -self-explanatory.
- Variances -errors identified which Resimac would not expect to occur but when corrected, the application is  considered  an  acceptable risk e.g.  incorrect  income used  but  when  corrected  application still  meets servicing.
- Breaches -applications which are considered to be outside of Resimac 's current risk appetite or regulatory documentation is incomplete e.g. incorrect income used, Application Privacy Statement &amp; Acknowledgement form unsigned.

The QA grading will determine whether the application will need to be declined as the risk to progress the loan to settlement is unacceptable to Resimac. The Head of Prime Lending Australia or Head of Specialist Lending Australia and General Manager Credit Risk and Asset Management will be notified by the QA reviewer via email of any application files falling into the 'Breach' category.

## 14.7 Process

The QA review process will consist of the following steps:

- Report will be generated of the previous day's process ed unconditional applications by the QA reviewer.
- Application files for review will be chosen e.g. Genworth DUA loans, high LVR loans etc.
- QA review of the soft copy loan files will be conducted with the completion of a QA review form and the QA register.
- Breaches  will  be  communicated  to  the  Head  of  Prime  Lending  Australia  or  Head  of  Specialist  Lending Australia and General Manager Credit Risk and Asset Management.

<!-- image -->

- Any application identified as a Breach will need further action by the Head of Prime Lending Australia or Head of Specialist Lending Australia i.e. application may need to be restructured to comply with Resimac 's policies or the application may need to be declined. If the loan is to be declined, the Head of Prime Lending Australia or Head of Specialist Lending Australia is to advise the Business Development Manager and the Mortgage Originator.
- The  Head  of  Prime  Lending  Australia  or  Head  of  Specialist  Lending  Australia  is  to  meet  with  the  staff member who originally assessed the application and discuss the QA findings.
- The  Head  of  Prime  Lending  Australia  or  Head  of  Specialist  Lending  Australia  is  to  advise  action  being undertaken in relation to any loan applications.

## 14.8 Reporting

A register of loan applications subject to QA review will be maintained by  the Quality Assurance Officer / Manager Credit Administration. This will provide the source of all quantitative reporting.

The Manager Credit Administration will include summary data of the month's QA activities in the Credit Risk and Asset Management Monthly report, with appropriate commentary.

Additional loan advances are permissible to existing loans subject to the particular loan qualifying under the relevant  Eligibility  Criteria  (to  be  defined  between  Mortgage  Manager  and  funder)  and  will  be  assessed according to the following criteria:

## 15.3 Substitution of Security

Any request to substitute a mortgaged property security for another real estate mortgage must be submitted to Resimac in writing (including being signed by all guarantors) and will be subject to a formal credit submission in accordance with the policies that apply for new money applications.

A substitution may entail the release of any of the security properties to a loan and the replacement with new security(s).

The new security(s) must fully comply with current underwriting policies (i.e. type, zoning, population density etc.).

A formal panel valuation is required for all new property security(s) and must satisfy all valuation liquidity risk parameters (i.e. location, comparable sales, environmental etc.).

Where no debt reduction is to be affected, the value of the proposed security(s) must be equal to or greater than the original value of the current security property(s) (i.e. the new LVR must be equal to or less than the current LVR).

Where the value of the proposed security(s) is less than the original value of the current security property(s), a proportionate debt reduction is to be affected to maintain the current LVR level.

New credit reference reports or financial information from the borrower(s) and guarantor(s) are not required.

Any mortgage security substitution approval will be subject to formal documentation which will require the consent of all borrowers and third-party security providers, including the guarantors. The considerations as to independent legal and financial advice and company guarantee requirements will apply as though the variation was a new facility.

NOTE: The  LMI  provider  must  provide  consent  to  the  substitution  of  security  request.  In  addition,  the mortgage insurer may also charge an administration fee.

## 15.5 Requests for Consent to Second Mortgage

The priority must indicate all amounts secured by the mortgaged asset / property including amounts owed contingently (i.e. pursuant to guarantees the mortgagor has provided on other facilities).

Resimac will not, without reasonable grounds, withhold its consent to the mortgagor granting a subsequent mortgage in favour of another financier.

Resimac 's consent will be subject to:

- Satisfactory conduct of the loan with any arrears to be adjusted prior to consent whereby Resimac will refuse consent where the loan has been subject to chronic arrears.
- Execution of the formal Resimac Deed of Priority.

All consents are to be approved in accordance with the delegations that apply for new money applications. No serviceability assessment is required for all second or subsequent mortgage requests.

In the event that Resimac does not consent to the second mortgage, the borrowers redraw will be inactivated and further advances will not be permitted until such time that a Deed of Priority is in place.

## FRINGE BENEFIT TAX EXEMPTION CHECKLIST

## Loan Benefit

Resimac Ltd, or a related corporation of Resimac, provided a loan to an employee or employee's associate

Yes

*An 'employee' is defined to mean a current, future or former employee. A current employee is person who is entitled to receive 'salary or wages'. i.e. payments to employees and company directors.

* An 'associate of employee' includes:

- the relatives of the employee including a de facto spouse

- a partner of a partnership

- a spouse or child of a partner

- a trustee of a trust estate, e.g. a trustee of a family trust

- a company that is effectively controlled by the employee or associates of the employee

## Exemptions

1 The rate of interest payable in respect of the loan (i.e. Interest Rate to Borrowers / Annual Percentage Rate) - (a) is specified in the Loan Agreement between RESIMAC and the Employee;

- (b) is not less than the actual rate of interest in respect of a similar arm's length loan made by Resimac at or about that time (i.e. settlement date), to a member of the public in the ordinary course of the business;

- (c) cannot be varied based on the employment terms/ service period;

- (d) Repayments and loan term of the loan is similar to an actual arm's length loan in the ordinary course of business; and

- (e) Fees and charges of the loan facility is similar to an actual arm's length loan in the ordinary course of business.
